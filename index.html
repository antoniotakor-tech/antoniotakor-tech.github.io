02 Research & concept 2026 Solo · Amsterdam

The Return Gap

Who is making the mess → Why the search fails
Role
Solo — research, framing, concept
Evidence
Eight street interviews, my own observation of the bins, and twelve interventions benchmarked across two countries
Made
A collection model and a costed first pilot
Status
Proposed. The pilot has not been run
Who I spoke to
Eight people in the street, over two days — Amsterdam Centraal, a canal-side park, along the canals. I went expecting complaints about rubbish. Not one person raised it unprompted. What broke the assumption came last: a resident of seventeen years said the bag gets torn open and half the time there is nothing in it.
What happens now
Home→ Street bin→ Bag torn open to look→ Often nothing in it

The search is blind, so it has to wreck the bag to find out. That is the mess.

What I proposed
Home→ Caught inside the building→ Returned for the deposit→ Money split three ways

Catch it at the front door and there is nothing left in a bag worth opening.

Bins across Amsterdam get torn open for deposit containers, and the city started pulling bins out in response. Nobody had asked what the person opening the bag was actually doing.

The mess is not carelessness; it is what a search leaves behind when it fails.

1

Discover

Two days of interviews in the street.

2

Define

Nobody raised it on their own. Everyone blamed a different group. Nobody had ever reported it.

3

Develop

Every attempt I could find anywhere acts on the public bin.

4

Deliver

Catch the containers inside the building instead. One building, three months, one number to watch.

04 Concept & validation 2025–26 Team of six

GrandPod

An app for lonely older people → A human on the other end
Role
Concept and validation, in a team of six
Evidence
26 concepts narrowed down, tested with people over 75, then assessed live
Made
A working physical prototype and a browser MVP
Who I spoke to
Around twenty people while narrowing down from 26 concepts, plus observation of how older people use public space. Tested with real participants over 75, then assessed live at a public prototype fair. The assumption that broke was the team's: we wanted to build an app for people who are defined by being shut out of apps., plus observation of how older people use public space. Tested with real participants over 75, then assessed live at a public prototype fair. The assumption that broke was the team's: we wanted to build an app for people who are defined by being shut out of apps.

Loneliness and digital exclusion among people over 75 who want to keep living on their own. The team's instinct was an app.

Voice first. A real person on the line, the machine behind it. These are people who are offline by definition. An app makes the barrier the product. Same reason AI does not go at the front: a synthetic voice asking a fraud-wary 80-year-old to trust it starts out losing.

We tested it with a cardboard box and a phone menu standing in for the real thing, first with people over 75 and then in front of assessors. What we learned there is why fear of being scammed decides what this group accepts from anyone, human or machine.
